## Why

`why` already stops at cut boundaries by default: its `cut-bounded` traversal names an unfinished
prior cut instead of expanding its members, and `--all` restores the complete transitive closure.
That change deliberately chose a cut rather than a depth bound and left a depth bound for the first
real deep chain of ordinary tasks. An adopting repository has now supplied one.

In that plan, `aplan why` on a task with two direct blockers returns 35 blocker records and 36
lines of human output under the `cut-bounded` traversal. The other 33 records are the ordinary-task
dependency chain behind one of the two direct blockers; no milestone occurs soon enough to fold it.
Generic shape: `ABC-100` is blocked by `ABC-050` and `ABC-099`, and `ABC-050` sits at the end of a
chain of 33 ordinary tasks, `ABC-001` to `ABC-033`, with no milestone along it.

The answer is graph-theoretically complete but operationally buries the two reasons an operator can
act on, and the JSON/MCP response spends context on prerequisites that can be expanded separately.
This is the boundary the cut-bounded design named but could not justify without a real adopter
example. Add a bounded ordinary-chain representation without making a partial answer look
exhaustive or removing the complete traversal.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] the default human, JSON, MCP, shared application and TUI `why` projections keep direct
      blockers prominent and bound a deep ordinary-task chain, while a caller can still request the
      complete transitive closure
- [ ] every bounded human or machine response, including the TUI inspector, explicitly names its
      traversal, each folded ordinary segment's entry task and omitted count, and the exact control
      that expands it; no count is presented as a complete blocker total
- [ ] ordinary chains shorter than the bound, milestone folding, mixed direct and transitive
      blockers, completed/dropped/undefined blockers, and cycle-refusal semantics remain unchanged
- [ ] tests reproduce the two-direct/35-total shape with a generic fixture and cover deterministic
      ordering, multiple deep branches, overlap/deduplication, human output, JSON, MCP parity,
      shared application and TUI projections, and `--all`
- [ ] README, HLD, changelog, generated agent guidance, upgrade guidance, behavior facts, TUI help
      and CLI/MCP help define the bound and compatibility behavior so an older complete-traversal
      client cannot silently claim the bounded contract

## Scope and compatibility

A first draft scoped the finding to CLI, JSON and MCP even though `aplan-app` owns the typed
work-discovery projection and the TUI exposes that projection with its own bounded/complete toggle.
A core-only bound could therefore make the terminal and protocol agree while the interactive
surface silently retained a third answer. The task therefore includes application and TUI
projection and rendering tests, plus their source paths.

Changing what the existing default traversal omits is a compatibility change even though the
`cut-bounded` label already exists. The task therefore declares breaking version impact and owns a
new behavior fact, generated agent guidance and the upgrade guide; an older binary that knows only
cut folding must not be mistaken for one that also bounds ordinary chains.
