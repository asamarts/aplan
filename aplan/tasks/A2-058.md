## Why

`aplan` ships a vocabulary mechanism with several axes (members, a cardinality of
`optional`/`at-most-one`/`at-least-one`/`exactly-one`, a status scope and a milestone scope) and
declares no family for its own plan. `.aplan.yml` has no `tag_families` key at all.

The pre-public plan showed what that costs when this was measured on 2026-08-26: **46 distinct tags
across 234 tasks, 17 of them used exactly once**:

```
correctness infra public quality security spec style testing
v0.1 v0.2 v0.3 v0.4 v0.5 v0.6 v0.7 v0.8 v0.9
```

Nine of those seventeen were version tags, one per milestone, carrying the identity the cut already
carries. The rest are a long tail that is not a vocabulary in any sense a reader could rely on:
nothing distinguishes `quality` from `correctness`, or `spec` from `docs`, and nothing ever will,
because nothing is looking.

This public plan has the same shape. On 2026-11-18 it held 65 distinct tags across 181 tasks, 18 of
them used exactly once. Six of those are cut labels that only their milestone carries
(`backlog`, `hardening`, `qualification`, `tui-quality`, `v0.7`, `v0.8`).

**This is the dogfooding gap that hides the design gaps.** One adopting repository declined
`tag_families` outright. Another mis-declared one and every layer accepted it, which is why a
declared family that no task carries is now reported (`APLAN029`). A third uses one. This
repository, which wrote the feature, has never had to live with it, so the questions an adopter
hits on day one (what belongs in a family, what cardinality is honest, what to do with the tail)
have never been asked here.

## What has to be decided

**Which tags are a vocabulary and which are noise.** `cross-cutting` is load-bearing and belongs to
the reserved set of five tags that change what the tool does (`milestone`, `release`,
`deprecation`, `removal`, `cross-cutting`, guarded by `APLAN051` and `APLAN052`), not to this
family. When this was measured, many tasks carrying it were also cut members, where the tag cannot
mean what `APLAN022` reads it as; deciding what each of those meant, and removing the tag where it
was describing the work rather than exempting it, was expected to be this task's largest single
edit. `adoption`, `cli`, `evidence`, `rules`, `output`, `config`, `interop`, `core` look like one
family describing what a task touches. The single-use tags are the decision: promoted into a
family, or deleted.

**Whether the version tags go.** A version tag on each milestone duplicates the cut the task already
is. If they carry nothing, deleting them is the honest outcome, and `aplan tag rename` (which
renames a tag on every task that carries it, as one change) is the tool for it.

**What cardinality is true here.** `at-most-one` on a "what it touches" family is a claim this plan
would have to satisfy, and finding out it does not is the point of the exercise rather than a reason
to weaken the declaration.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `.aplan.yml` declares at least one family with a cardinality, and this plan satisfies it
- [ ] every single-use tag in this plan is promoted into a family or removed, with version tags
      decided explicitly
- [ ] whatever was awkward about declaring a family here is written down, because that is the part an
      adopter meets and this repository has never seen
