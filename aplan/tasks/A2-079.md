## Why

`aplan critical-path` opens with one sentence holding two measurements:

    critical path: 22.0 days across 11 task(s), 6.0d of it not yet done across 3 task(s)

The first is the rows beneath it: eleven of them, and their efforts sum to 22.0. The second is
a **different chain**: the critical path computed over the unfinished work alone, which is not a
subset of the first. In the plan this was measured on, the printed rows held two tasks that were
not `done` (1.0d and 2.0d), totalling 3.0d. The clause says 6.0d across three.

Both numbers are right. The sentence is not: "**of it**" claims a part-whole relationship the
second number does not have, and a reader who checks the arithmetic against the rows (which is the
natural thing to do with a total printed above a list) finds it does not add up and has no way to
tell which of the two is wrong. Nothing names the third task, so there is nothing to reconcile it
against.

This clause has been audited once already, when it was found to compare the two numbers with
`f64::EPSILON`. That audit fixed *when* the clause appears and left *what it claims* alone.

An adopting repository reproduced the non-degenerate case twice. For one checkpoint, the printed
full chain had four live tasks and 8.5 days while the unprinted remaining-work chain had ten tasks
and 24.5 days; a second checkpoint showed the same split. Its independent calculation identified
the exact second chain, so this is a wording and projection defect rather than bad arithmetic.

## What to decide

- Whether the remaining chain is worth printing at all, or whether the honest version of this
  is the unfinished part of the chain shown (which would make "of it" true and the number
  3.0d).
- If it stays, the wording that does not imply containment, and whether its rows are shown or
  at least its task ids named, since a number a reader cannot reconcile is worse than one that
  is absent.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] the opening sentence's two numbers can be reconciled against what the command printed,
      either because the second measures the rows shown or because it names its own chain
- [ ] a plan where the two chains differ is the fixture, so the case this task exists for is
      the one under test rather than the degenerate one where they agree
- [ ] the machine-readable surfaces carry the same answer as the human one, by the parity the
      format census already requires
- [ ] the changed interpretation of the existing command has one proved behaviour name, release
      note and upgrade statement
