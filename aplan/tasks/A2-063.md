## Why

The duration model (a cut's duration is derived from its effort and one declared throughput,
ADR-0023) tells adopters to measure throughput, but the task schema retains only the estimate used
to plan work. One adopting repository could reconstruct one cut only from a private agent-session
event ledger; without a durable per-task shape, its next re-plan would repeat source-specific
archaeology and could silently omit correction work or count overlapping sessions twice.

This is not automatically an argument for a stopwatch inside aplan. Actual time may be sensitive,
and repositories may measure focus, elapsed time and external waits differently. It is an argument
that the tool must make an explicit portability decision instead of documenting a measurement
step whose inputs its model cannot represent or validate.

A2-108 (`ADR-0074`) deliberately does not answer this question: its `closed_at` is one lifecycle
transition claim used for ordering completed tasks, not an interval, duration or measure of effort.
Neither field can be derived honestly from the other, and the completion-history work must not make
this task look settled.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] decide in an ADR whether actual task time is core task data, a configured extension, an
      evidence kind, or deliberately repository-owned; account for privacy, append/correction
      semantics, multiple intervals, focused versus external-wait time and estimate immutability
- [ ] if the data is modelled, define one versioned schema and make every reader/writer/export
      surface preserve it; reject invalid timestamps, non-positive or overlapping focus intervals,
      duplicate data and totals that disagree with derived intervals
- [ ] if the data remains outside aplan, make the measurement guidance say what portable evidence
      a consumer must retain and expose a documented extension boundary that does not require a
      second task-status parser
- [ ] keep measured focus/elapsed/wait data semantically separate from A2-108's close-transition
      chronology in schemas, reports and migration guidance
- [ ] prove the decision against a complete-cut fixture of the reported shape: every member,
      including dropped and corrective work, must be enumerable, and estimate, focus, calendar
      elapsed and external wait must not collapse into one number
- [ ] add migration/upgrade guidance and tests for every structured surface the accepted decision
      changes
