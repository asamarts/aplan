## Why

A milestone in another milestone's `blocked_by` is not containment. ADR-0008 reads the outer list
as membership, and ADR-0013 propagates the inner milestone edge to every outer member: nesting a
cut sequences all of that work after the inner cut. One adopting repository lost 16 ready tasks
when four young cuts were nested this way, affecting 25 tasks in total. The shape:

```yaml
# M-outer
blocked_by: [M-inner, ABC-001, ABC-002]
```

`ABC-001` and `ABC-002` are members of `M-outer`, and the `M-inner` entry makes both of them wait
until `M-inner` is done.

`APLAN007` exposes the mistake only after the outer cut has finished members, and then reports one
finding per finished sibling ("its cut follows `M-inner`, which is `todo`"). The same adopter saw
eight findings naming unchanged completed tasks for one mistaken nesting edge. On a young cut with
no completed member, the identical edge is valid and silent. The model is coherent; the missing
part is an explicit explanation at the mutation and diagnostic points where an author is deciding
whether the edge means membership or sequence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] adoption and model documentation state that putting one milestone in another milestone's
      `blocked_by` sequences every outer member after the inner milestone
- [ ] the relevant mutation result or refusal describes that consequence before an author must
      infer it from many `APLAN007` findings
- [ ] where `APLAN007` reports this shape, its message names the nesting edge and the decision once
      rather than making unchanged done siblings look like the subject
- [ ] a legitimate milestone-to-milestone dependency remains supported without an interactive
      confirmation prompt or nondeterministic policy
- [ ] tests pin both the silent legitimate edge and the diagnostic-quality case with several
      finished siblings
