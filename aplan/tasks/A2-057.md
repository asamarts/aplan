## Why

`ready --explain` answers which workable tasks the plan ranks first and why, and
`graph --format text` answers how a selected subgraph is connected. Neither produces the
compact "what should happen next, and what does it unlock?" picture. In a large adopting plan,
expanding downstream from one task rendered most of the product graph with 137 crossing
edges, while the useful view combined several independently ready fronts, their near
releases, one parked convergence blocker and one just-completed context task. It had to be
drawn by hand.

The missing operation is selection, not drawing: `--focus` decorates one node without
changing scope, equality selectors cannot name an ad hoc set of ids, and one `--reach` query
expands one cone. The tool must not invent an opaque priority score either: tool-derived
seeds reuse the one ready ranking, and operator-selected seeds are labelled as selected.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] one documented invocation reproduces a committed fixture's multi-front next-work view without
      editing the plan or including the entire downstream cone
- [ ] tool-ranked seeds come from the existing ready order; selected seeds stay visibly operator-
      selected; no second priority score is introduced
- [ ] the bounded neighbourhood rule is deterministic and documented, preserves whole-plan readiness,
      distinguishes graph-blocked from parked work, and reports omitted nodes and crossing edges
- [ ] text, ASCII, Mermaid, DOT and JSON renderings consume one projection and agree on nodes and
      edges
