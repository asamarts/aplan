## Why

On 2026-11-18 the full local gate failed seven real-terminal tests while another project's
mutation testing held the host at a load of about 90 on 24 cores:
`tui_portability::shared_real_pty_lifecycle_handles_focus_resize_and_tiny_terminals`,
`tui_portability::portable_control_c_restores_terminal_modes`,
`tui_portability::external_editor_handoff_restores_modes_and_refreshes_identity`,
`tui_journey::terminating_signals_restore_the_terminal_and_keep_the_exit_reason`,
`tui_journey::overdue_probe_recovers_after_a_long_provider_in_a_real_terminal`,
`tui_journey::mutations_claim_start_edit_dependencies_add_and_close_in_a_real_terminal` and
`tui_work_action_parity::cli_and_typed_tui_claim_and_release_cover_every_status`. Each failed
after 22 to 46 seconds. All seven passed when rerun alone at the same load. The last one failed
its "adapter clocks diverged" check: the CLI and TUI halves stamp their claims with the wall
clock, the check allows two seconds between them, and under that load they ran 19 seconds apart.

A gate that fails on contention teaches its readers to rerun until green, which is how a real
TUI regression gets waved through.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] the real-terminal tests share a nextest test group whose concurrency keeps them within
      their deadlines on a loaded host, or their deadlines are derived from measured headroom
- [ ] the work-action parity tests compare claim times against the interval each half ran in,
      or use an injected clock, rather than allowing a fixed two seconds between the halves
- [ ] the seven tests above pass in a full suite run while the host is held at twice its core
      count in load, and the run is recorded in this task
