## Why

`aplan` resolves `blocked_by` against the plan, `traces` against a document section, and a
`commit` citation against a diff. It does not read a **status word in prose**, and a plan's prose
is full of them. One adopting repository wrote two instances in one increment, a few hours apart.
With the ids made generic:

```console
$ grep -n 'ABC-015' aplan/tasks/ABC-050.md
94:Not folded into `ABC-015`, which is `todo` and whose criteria are about the macOS runner
$ grep -n 'ABC-015' CONTRIBUTING.md
71:`ABC-015` and `ABC-016`. Until those land, **verify cross-platform changes locally** and say
```

`ABC-015` closed two hours after the first of those sentences was written. Both went stale in that
moment, and `check` plus `verify` stayed green across the change, correctly: `ABC-050` was `todo`,
nothing it cited had rotted, and the graph was intact. What decayed was a sentence.

**It is resolvable, which is what separates it from a neighbouring finding that was declined.**
That one was a body asserting a remote API's state at a moment, with nothing for a tool to go and
re-resolve, and it was declined for exactly that reason. This claim is about a task in the same
plan, in the same directory, whose status is already in memory while the body making the claim is
parsed.

The nearest machinery that exists is not close. `aplan-interop`'s `report_references` scans a body
for `SPEC-`, `ADR-` and `RFC-` prefixes and reports them as prose kept out of `traces`. It runs
during a one-time import, it does not look for task ids, and nothing like it runs in `check`.

**The signal is an id plus a status word, not an id.** Cross-references in prose are normal and
wanted, and a diagnostic that objected to all of them would be switched off in a day. The forms
that carry a status claim are countable: "is `todo`", "which is done", "still open", "is blocked",
"until those land", "not yet started". A proximity window plus a disagreement with the real status
is the whole rule.

The adopter corrected both sentences in place, each with the date and the reason. That is the
consumer's half of this, and it does not substitute for the check.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] a task body that asserts another task's status, where the assertion disagrees with that
      task's actual status, is reported with the id, the asserted word and the real one
- [ ] a bare cross-reference carrying no status word is **not** reported, and a fixture proves it,
      because a rule that has to be suppressed is worth nothing
- [ ] the proximity window and the status vocabulary are stated where a reader can find them, so a
      plan that writes "ABC-123 is the importer" is not read as a status claim
- [ ] the diagnostic carries an `APLAN` code, a `CHANGELOG.md` entry and a line in the rule
      reference, which `cargo xtask check-docs` already requires of a shipped rule
- [ ] a decision is recorded about documents **outside** the plan directory. Both instances were
      real and the second was in `CONTRIBUTING.md`, which no `aplan` command reads. Either the rule
      covers configured prose paths, or this task says plainly that it does not, and why
