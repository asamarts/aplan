## Why

`traces` is the feature one adopting repository adopted aplan for: 33 tasks anchored into 148
existing plan documents, so moving a heading is reported rather than silently breaking a link.

Aiming it is guesswork. A slugifier written from the obvious rules disagreed on punctuation and 13
of 36 anchors were refused. For example, a heading `## Progress (v5.0, updated 2026-07-18)`
slugifies to `progress-v50-updated-2026-07-18`, while a slugifier that turns `.` into `-` writes
`progress-v5-0-updated-2026-07-18`, which resolves against nothing. `APLAN008`'s fix message says
what the anchor must become, one failure at a time, and nothing prints the anchors a document
*does* offer.

The adopter's workaround was worse than the gap: a script that only accepts headings matching
`[A-Za-z0-9 .]+`, so eight tasks got no `traces` at all because every heading in their document
carries punctuation, and the other 33 are anchored to whichever clean heading came first rather
than the relevant one. A feature that works and cannot be aimed gets used badly or not at all.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] a command prints the anchors a document offers, in the form `traces` wants
- [ ] it covers headings and table rows, the two things `APLAN008` resolves
- [ ] `docs/` shows it being used to author a `traces` entry
