## Why

One adopter declared complete question, spike and risk vocabularies as tag families and used
`require: at-most-one`, but that requirement constrains the number of family members on each task.
It neither requires every declared member to appear nor prevents the same member from appearing on
two different tasks. A consumer that needs one primary owner for every catalog entry must parse the
whole plan and maintain a second global uniqueness check.

A generic reproducer:

```yaml
# .aplan.yml
tag_families:
  question:
    members: [q-latency, q-cost, q-scale]
    require: at-most-one
```

`ABC-001` and `ABC-002` both carry `q-latency`, `ABC-003` carries `q-scale`, and no task carries
`q-cost`. `aplan check` is silent: every task carries at most one member, and the family as a
whole is carried, so `APLAN029` has nothing to say. The adopter wanted each member exactly once,
which here means one duplicate (`q-latency`) and one missing member (`q-cost`).

The existing per-task cardinality remains useful and must keep its meaning. This task adds an
opt-in, independently named plan-wide member-usage policy rather than overloading `require` or
making unused vocabulary universally erroneous.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A tag-family declaration can opt into plan-wide per-member cardinality sufficient to express
      each declared member appearing exactly once, without changing the current task-local
      `require` semantics or existing configurations.
- [ ] Scope and diagnostics are explicit: a violation names the family, member, count, and every
      carrying task; missing and duplicate members are distinguishable and deterministic.
- [ ] `check`, add/bulk-add post-state validation, and tag mutations apply the policy atomically,
      while reports and machine output do not falsely imply task-local cardinality provides
      global coverage.
- [ ] Config parsing, generated schema, facts/capability surfaces, README, changelog, and
      upgrading guidance document the distinction and migration path.
- [ ] Unit plus end-to-end fires/silent cases cover missing, duplicate, scoped-out, milestone,
      status, overlapping-family, mutation-refusal, and backward-compatible near-miss behavior.
