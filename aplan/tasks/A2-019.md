## Why

The public plan began at the sanitized import with fifteen tasks, all release and
infrastructure work. The open product work stayed in the pre-public maintainer ledger, and the
eighteen issues filed against v0.6.0 (#18 to #36) had no plan task. `aplan ready` on the public
plan therefore reported nothing to do while more than a hundred known defects and features
waited elsewhere, which is the drift a plan validator exists to prevent.

The maintainer decided (2026-11-17) to move all open product work into this plan at once, each
task rewritten so that its reason stands without access to private material, and to file
every open issue as one or more tasks, splitting bundled issues and putting a decision task
in front of each change that contradicts an earlier recorded decision.

The plan is sequenced to state priority: `v0.7` (security, the YAML parser, CI health and the
findings that make a gate lie) precedes `v0.8` (the remaining correctness defects and the
most reported adoption failures), which precedes the thematic cuts.

Untestable: this is plan authoring. `aplan check` and `aplan verify` judging the result is the
proof, and no test fails if a task file is reverted.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] every open issue on the public repository is named by at least one task, and bundled issues are
      split so each problem can be closed on its own
- [ ] every migrated task's reason, reproducer and criteria are self-contained, and the public history
      privacy audit passes
- [ ] every task belongs to exactly one cut, `check` and `verify` pass with zero warnings, and `ready`
      lists the v0.7 work
- [ ] the roadmap narrative states the cut order and why
