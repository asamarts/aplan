## Why

One adopting plan put seven security-review gates in `ratified_by`. Its first inspection concluded
that task detail omitted both those edges and `prefer_after`, but that conclusion came from a stale
build: the shipped task inspector work already made outgoing `ratified_by` and `prefer_after`
typed `Related` values in CLI JSON and MCP, and the human CLI and TUI render both in relationship
sections.

The audit did confirm a narrower completeness gap. A task shows who must ratify it, but a ratifier
does not show the tasks it ratifies. Loaded neighbours also carry identity, status and
availability but not their titles, so a reviewer must open every id to understand the review set.
In a plan where `ABC-001` and `ABC-002` both declare `ratified_by: [ABC-009]`, `aplan show ABC-001`
names `ABC-009` as its ratifier, but `aplan show ABC-009` says nothing about the two results it
ratifies.

This is materially different from readiness: `ratified_by` constrains whether a `done` claim is
valid, never whether work can start. Task detail must expose both directions without
misclassifying either one as a producer dependency. Outgoing relationships already exist; incoming
`ratifies` and loaded-neighbour titles are the work here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The shared typed show projection preserves outgoing `ratified_by` and `prefer_after`, adds
      reverse incoming `ratifies`, and carries title with identity, status and availability for
      every loaded related task. Missing and unloadable neighbours remain explicit and never
      acquire invented metadata.
- [ ] CLI and TUI task detail render `ratified by`, `ratifies`, and `prefers after` as distinct
      relationship sections, separate from producer blockers and releases; no label or placement
      implies that these relationships change readiness.
- [ ] Human CLI, CLI JSON, MCP task detail, and the TUI consume the same projection. An executable
      parity census fails if a surface omits a new relationship member or reconstructs reverse
      edges independently.
- [ ] Behavioral, parity, reducer, and narrow/no-color golden tests cover a task awaiting a
      ratifier, a task ratifying more than one result, a preferred review order, loaded titles,
      missing and unloadable neighbours, and deterministic ordering.
- [ ] Because this widens existing `show` and task-inspector behavior, the implementation adds a
      proved `Behaviour` capability in the same commit. README, HLD, TUI design, facts/changelog
      and upgrade guidance describe the complete projection and preserve the rule that
      ratification constrains completion, not readiness.
