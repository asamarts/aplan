## Why

One adopting repository authored a repository-wide audit task with `paths: ['**']`. Both `check`
and `verify` were green while the task was live. Only after a 114-file implementation commit
existed did `close` reveal that a universal glob can never bind that commit to the specific work,
and refuse it with `APLAN012`.

The close-time refusal is correct and must remain. The gap is timing: the task was written before
the work specifically so an invalid ownership claim could be rejected cheaply. A structural
warning can identify a universal pattern without pretending the future commit is known.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] a live non-milestone task whose `paths` include a universal catch-all receives an early
      diagnostic explaining that commit evidence cannot prove relevance
- [ ] patterns that are broad but not universal are not guessed at by the rule; breadth remains
      `aplan paths`/vacuity's measured question
- [ ] terminal historical tasks and non-commit evidence keep their documented semantics
- [ ] the close-time `APLAN012` refusal remains fail-closed and uses the same universal-pattern
      predicate as the early diagnostic
- [ ] the new rule has unit message/instruction assertions plus an end-to-end fires/silent pair
