## Why

An `add --from` batch in an adopting repository declared one task twice for the same milestone:
once through the batch entry's `cut` field and once in that same milestone's `blocked_by`. Generic
reproducer: a batch entry for `ABC-001` with `cut: M-one`, plus an entry that writes `M-one` with
`blocked_by: [ABC-001]`. APLAN022 correctly refused the state, but it said the task "belongs to 2
cuts" and told the author to find a second milestone. There was only one distinct cut; the defect
was two declarations of the same membership edge.

The rule must retain its one-cut invariant while carrying the actual subjects its predicate found.
This is the same diagnostic-integrity boundary as a collection rule naming the wrong member: an
edge count must not be formatted as a distinct-cut count.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] APLAN022 distinguishes one cut declared twice from membership in two distinct cuts, naming
      the repeated declaration sites or the distinct cut ids that the predicate actually found
- [ ] the duplicate declaration refusal gives an actionable repair for `add --from` input and for
      an already-written plan without implying that an unseen milestone exists
- [ ] one declaration, the same cut declared twice, two distinct cuts, repeated entries inside one
      milestone, release/checkpoint borrowing and milestone-to-milestone edges have separate tests
- [ ] human, JSON, agent instruction and mutation-gate output agree on the subject and count; the
      message remains deterministic when three or more declarations or cuts are present
- [ ] the changed meaning of existing APLAN022 earns a proved `Behaviour`, and the rule catalogue,
      changelog and facts state the distinction without weakening one-cut membership
