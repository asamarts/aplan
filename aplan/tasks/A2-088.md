## Why

Builders already know the keymap and data model, so dogfood alone cannot reveal whether the UI
is intuitive. A release decision needs observed first-use and expert workflows with task-level
dispositions, not a satisfaction claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] at least five human participants include unfamiliar users and experienced terminal/tool users;
      language-model actor runs are reported separately and do not count toward this minimum
- [ ] sessions cover missing-plan onboarding, plan health, finding diagnosis, blocker discovery,
      graph navigation, planning and evidence-gated close, including a deliberately delayed startup
      and slow CLI operation whose progress can be interpreted and disabled
- [ ] graph sessions measure whether participants can find a task, trace a blocker and a dependent,
      identify the critical route, explain omitted work and recover from an off-screen selection;
      at least one fixture's full graph must be too wide and require bounded-window traversal;
      errors and completion time are recorded separately for CLI text and TUI interaction where
      the same question exists
- [ ] graph sessions begin at the plan/phase overview and measure whether participants can identify
      the next milestone, drill to one task, move by dependency layer and direct relation, explain
      an aggregate edge, change ordering without mistaking it for dependency order, and return to
      the prior overview without losing orientation
- [ ] the layered candidate is compared with the prior lane baseline on path accuracy, wrong turns,
      backtracking, completion time and explanation quality; visual preference is recorded
      separately and cannot compensate for a comprehension regression
- [ ] completion, errors, help use, backtracking and qualitative confusion are recorded without
      inventing a universal score
- [ ] task-summary scan questions measure whether identity, status/readiness, ownership/claim,
      placement and next actions are found without reading every row; ask each participant what the
      selected work is, whether it can start, who owns and currently holds it, where it belongs and
      which detail/action they would open next, recording answer time, wrong answers, backtracking
      and whether they read the inspector sequentially before answering
- [ ] accessibility and narrow/short-terminal sessions are included rather than inferred from
      snapshots, and cover list/detail switching, overlays and graph continuation controls
- [ ] every observation is fixed, filed as a task with priority, or declined with a reason before
      release review
- [ ] the report separates measured behavior from participant preference and records the tested
      build identity
- [ ] participation is consensual, observations are anonymized, and fixtures contain no participant
      secrets or private repository content

## Why it is `untestable`

This task records human usability observations and their dispositions. Automated tests can
protect fixes those observations cause, but cannot prove that an interface is intuitive.
Synthetic actor or evaluator runs, as defined in `docs/design/tui-ai-evaluation.md`, can preflight
the study and discover candidate failures, but cannot satisfy any participant criterion or supply
a release verdict.
