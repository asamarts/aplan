## Why

One adopting repository converted eleven work-stream tasks into cuts containing 48 slice tasks.
Every conversion needed the same coordinated edits: turn the old task into a milestone, lift it
out of its former cut, move each member out of its former cut, and decide which existing
`blocked_by` edges are real dependencies rather than future membership. The diagnostics were
correct, but arrived one layer at a time across three or four rejected states. The adopter wrote a
30-line helper to make the operation atomic.

This is not permission to guess at an ambiguous edge. One of those conversions found a stale real
dependency that had to be removed; a mutation must preview or refuse that decision rather than
reinterpret it silently.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] one command can promote an existing non-terminal task to a milestone and move explicitly
      named members into it as one validated compare-and-swap change
- [ ] the promoted task is removed from any former cut and each member is removed from its former
      cut, with every changed membership named in the preview/result
- [ ] pre-existing `blocked_by` edges on the promoted task are classified or explicitly supplied;
      the command refuses ambiguity rather than treating all of them as members
- [ ] paths, effort, evidence and other fields forbidden on a milestone are previewed and require
      an explicit disposition rather than being discarded
- [ ] a failure leaves every task byte-identical, and contention cannot produce a half-converted
      graph
